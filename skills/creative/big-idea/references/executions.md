# Executions

How to write the pieces once the idea exists. The idea decides what to say; this file covers how each medium says it.

## Headlines

The headline does most of the selling (Ogilvy: five times as many people read it as the body). Write at least 40 for any headline that matters, and expect the best to come late.

- **Specific beats general.** A number, a name, a detail ("the loudest noise… comes from the electric clock") beats an adjective.
- **Promise or provoke.** A headline either offers a benefit the reader wants or opens a question they need closed.
- **Pair it with the visual.** In Bernbach's teams, word and picture make one idea; the headline says what the picture can't, and the reverse. A headline that describes its own picture is wasted.
- **Read it aloud.** Rhythm, plain words, and conversational syntax.

Headline types worth trying, one or more each:

| Type | Shape |
|------|-------|
| News | "Introducing…", "At last…", what is new and why it matters |
| Fact | The buried fact, verbatim |
| Question | One the reader can't answer without reading on |
| How / why | "How a…", "Why…" with a specific outcome |
| Testimonial | A real or archetypal voice, in quotes |
| Command | An imperative that tells the reader to do or think something |
| Contrast | Two halves that collide ("Think small." against a culture of big) |
| Admission | The flaw, said plainly |
| Twist | A familiar phrase with one word changed |
| Understatement | Saying less than the claim deserves, so the reader supplies the rest |

## Body copy

- Open with the most interesting sentence you have; the reader decides in one line whether to go on.
- One idea per paragraph, facts over adjectives, short sentences mixed with long ones.
- Write to one person, in the second person, the way you would talk to them across a table.
- Answer the objections the reader is forming.
- End on the action you want and the reason to take it now.
- Long copy works when every sentence earns the next; short copy works when the idea is already complete in the headline and visual.

## Taglines

A tagline compresses the proposition into words people can repeat. Strong ones do one of these:

- **State the proposition** — "We try harder."
- **Command** — "Just do it." "Think different."
- **Ask** — "Got milk?"
- **Hold a truth** — "You're not you when you're hungry."
- **Promise a feeling** — "Good things come to those who wait."

Test: would someone say it in conversation, with or without the brand name? Does it still make sense on the side of a truck, on its own?

## Print and outdoor

- One idea, one image, as few words as possible. Outdoor gets about three seconds.
- Leave the empty space that makes the one thing seen (Think Small used most of the page for white space).
- For a serial format (Absolut, *The Economist*), define the template's fixed parts and write at least five fills to prove it has legs.
- Consider the location as part of the ad: what does it mean to put this here?

## Film

Describe the hero film as a story in three to six sentences: the opening image, the turn, the ending, and where the brand enters. Name the length and channel. For a full shot-by-shot script, hand the concept to the `video-script` skill.

## Promotions and stunts

A promotion is an idea with mechanics. Write it as a sequence:

1. **Hook** — what the audience first sees or hears, and where.
2. **Ask** — what they have to do. The smaller the ask, the bigger the participation.
3. **Reward** — what they get, and why it is worth it to them, not just to the brand.
4. **Story** — what they tell other people afterward, and the screenshot, photo, or line they share.
5. **Proof** — how the brand's proposition is demonstrated by the act of taking part.
6. **Rules and risk** — limits, cost exposure, legal needs (sweepstakes law varies by region), how it could be gamed or mocked.

Mechanics to choose from, each strongest when the mechanic itself proves the proposition:

| Mechanic | Use when |
|----------|----------|
| Sampling or free trial | The product wins once tried (Hopkins) |
| Challenge or head-to-head test | The product beats a rival in a fair comparison (Pepsi Challenge) |
| Personalization | Names, data, or creations make the product personal (Share a Coke, Wrapped) |
| Trade or sacrifice | A playful cost that reveals value (Whopper Sacrifice) |
| Location or geofence | Place makes the joke or the point (Whopper Detour) |
| Limited edition or scarcity | Rarity creates urgency and collectors |
| Collect and complete | Repeat purchase over time (game pieces, stamps) |
| User creation | The audience makes the content (Draw Ketchup) |
| Public demonstration | A live act proves the claim (Epic Split, Will It Blend?) |
| Costly gesture | Giving something up proves sincerity (Don't Buy This Jacket) |

## Social and earned

Check the idea against STEPPS (Berger): does sharing it make the sharer look good, does something in daily life trigger it, does it provoke high-arousal emotion, is participation visible, is there practical value, does the brand travel inside a story? An idea that fails all six depends on paid media to be seen.

Write the shareable artifact explicitly: the post, the screenshot, the sound, the format others can copy.
