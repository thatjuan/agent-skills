# Cases

Landmark campaigns, each broken into the problem it faced, the insight, the leap, and why it worked. Use them in step 4 as fuel (what move solved a problem like this one?) and in step 7 as the **ancestor** for each concept. Take the move, not the surface: a new idea that copies an ancestor's look is a pastiche, while one that reuses its logic on fresh raw material is craft.

Dates and agencies are given where well documented; treat them as context, not as claims to put in client work without checking.

## The creative revolution, 1950s–1970s

**Volkswagen, "Think Small" and "Lemon"** — DDB, 1959–60
- *Problem:* A small, odd-looking German car in an America of chrome and tailfins, fifteen years after the war.
- *Insight:* Americans were tired of being oversold. Honesty would be a novelty.
- *Leap:* A tiny car in a sea of white space with "Think Small." A pristine Beetle headlined "Lemon," because an inspector had rejected it over a blemished chrome strip on the glove compartment.
- *Why it worked:* Admitting the flaw made every other claim credible. Self-deprecating wit respected the reader.
- *Lenses:* Flaw as virtue, Break the wallpaper.

**Avis, "We Try Harder"** — DDB, 1962
- *Problem:* Avis was the distant No. 2 to Hertz and losing money.
- *Insight:* The underdog is more motivated, and people root for the underdog.
- *Leap:* "Avis is only No. 2 in rent a cars. So why go with us? We try harder."
- *Why it worked:* It turned a weakness into a promise of service and gave employees a standard to live up to.
- *Lenses:* Number two, Flaw as virtue.

**Hathaway shirts, "The man in the Hathaway shirt"** — Ogilvy, 1951
- *Problem:* A small shirtmaker with a tiny budget in a category of identical ads.
- *Insight:* A detail that raises a question makes readers stop and supply a story.
- *Leap:* An aristocratic model wearing an eye patch.
- *Why it worked:* Story appeal. The eye patch became the brand's signature for years.
- *Lenses:* Character, Showmanship.

**Rolls-Royce, "At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock"** — Ogilvy, 1958
- *Problem:* Saying "luxury" and "quality" in a way readers would believe.
- *Insight:* One concrete fact proves more than any adjective.
- *Leap:* A line lifted from an engineering report, followed by long copy full of more facts.
- *Why it worked:* Specificity is credibility.
- *Lenses:* The buried fact.

**Schlitz beer** — Claude Hopkins, early 1900s
- *Problem:* Schlitz was fifth in its market; every brewer claimed "purity."
- *Insight:* Visiting the brewery, Hopkins found steam-cleaned bottles, filtered air, and artesian wells. The brewers considered all of it standard.
- *Leap:* Tell the process in detail.
- *Why it worked:* Standard to the brewer was astonishing to the drinker, and the first to say it owned it.
- *Lenses:* Preemptive claim, The buried fact.

**M&M's, "Melts in your mouth, not in your hand"** — Ted Bates (Rosser Reeves), 1954
- *Problem:* Selling candy-coated chocolate.
- *Insight:* The candy shell was a real, unique benefit.
- *Leap:* The USP in seven words, demonstrated on screen with two hands.
- *Lenses:* The USP in seven words, Demonstration.

**Green Giant, "Harvested in the moonlight"** — Leo Burnett
- *Problem:* Peas are peas.
- *Insight:* The peas were packed quickly after picking, sometimes overnight.
- *Leap:* Turn a logistics fact into romance.
- *Lenses:* Inherent drama.

**De Beers, "A Diamond Is Forever"** — N.W. Ayer, 1947
- *Problem:* Making diamonds an expected part of an engagement.
- *Leap:* Four words linking the stone to permanence and love.
- *Why it worked:* It built a ritual, not a sale, and implied a diamond should never be resold.
- *Lenses:* Cultural artifact, Own a word.

**Braniff, "The End of the Plain Plane"** — Wells Rich Greene, 1965
- *Problem:* A regional airline indistinguishable from the rest.
- *Leap:* Paint the planes in bright colors and dress the crew in designer uniforms.
- *Why it worked:* The product change was the ad.
- *Lenses:* Change the product, Break the wallpaper.

**Cadbury Smash, the Martians** — BMP, 1974
- *Problem:* Instant mashed potato looked like a lazy compromise.
- *Leap:* Robot Martians laughing hysterically at primitive Earthlings who peel, boil, and mash potatoes by hand.
- *Why it worked:* Entertainment made the brand famous; the joke reframed the old way as the absurd one.
- *Lenses:* Showmanship, Character, The opposite.

**I ❤ NY** — Wells Rich Greene with Milton Glaser's logo, 1977
- *Problem:* New York had a reputation for crime and decline.
- *Leap:* A declaration of love anyone could wear.
- *Why it worked:* Simple enough to become a cultural artifact, copied worldwide.
- *Lenses:* Cultural artifact, Identity.

**Pepsi Challenge** — 1975
- *Problem:* No. 2 to Coca-Cola.
- *Leap:* Public blind taste tests, filmed.
- *Why it worked:* The promotion was a demonstration, and the leader could not answer it in kind.
- *Lenses:* Blind test, Number two, Demonstration.

## The 1980s and 1990s

**Apple, "1984"** — Chiat/Day, directed by Ridley Scott, 1984
- *Problem:* Launching the Macintosh against IBM.
- *Insight:* Frame computing as freedom against conformity.
- *Leap:* An Orwellian film in which a runner smashes Big Brother's screen; it aired nationally once, during the Super Bowl.
- *Why it worked:* An enemy, a metaphor, and a single event that became news in itself.
- *Lenses:* The enemy, Metaphor, One shot only.

**Wendy's, "Where's the beef?"** — Dancer Fitzgerald Sample, 1984
- *Leap:* An elderly woman peering at a giant bun with a tiny patty.
- *Why it worked:* Attacks rivals on a concrete fact, and the line entered politics as a catchphrase.
- *Lenses:* The enemy, Character.

**The Economist, red posters** — Abbott Mead Vickers, from 1988
- *Leap:* White type on red, one witty line: "'I never read The Economist.' Management trainee. Aged 42."
- *Why it worked:* A fixed template that flatters the reader's intelligence; each poster is an event.
- *Lenses:* Format serial, Identity.

**Nike, "Just Do It"** — Wieden+Kennedy, 1988
- *Problem:* Reaching beyond hardcore athletes.
- *Insight:* Everyone has a voice telling them not to bother.
- *Leap:* Three words addressed to anyone, inspired by a condemned man's last words, "Let's do it."
- *Why it worked:* It made the customer the hero and became a personal motto.
- *Lenses:* Customer as hero, Unsaid truth.

**Absolut Vodka, the bottle series** — TBWA, from 1980
- *Leap:* Every ad is the bottle shape plus a two-word headline: "Absolut Perfection," then cities, artists, puns.
- *Why it worked:* A template that ran for decades and became collectible.
- *Lenses:* Hero the product, Format serial, Visual pun.

**California Milk Processor Board, "Got Milk?"** — Goodby Silverstein & Partners, 1993
- *Problem:* Milk consumption was falling, and "milk is good for you" was ignored.
- *Insight:* People think about milk only when they run out of it.
- *Leap:* Deprivation: a history buff who could win a radio contest but can't say "Aaron Burr" through a mouth full of peanut butter.
- *Lenses:* Deprivation, Loss frame.

**Apple, "Think Different"** — TBWA\Chiat\Day, 1997
- *Problem:* Apple near collapse; the brand needed to remind people what it stood for.
- *Leap:* Portraits of Einstein, Gandhi, Picasso, and others: "Here's to the crazy ones."
- *Why it worked:* It sold identity, not specs, to employees as much as customers.
- *Lenses:* Identity, The enemy (conformity).

**Guinness, "Surfer"** — AMV BBDO, 1999
- *Problem:* The slow pour annoyed drinkers in busy pubs.
- *Insight:* Waiting builds anticipation.
- *Leap:* "Good things come to those who wait," with surfers waiting for the perfect wave of white horses.
- *Lenses:* Flaw as virtue, Showmanship.

**Hotmail, "PS: I love you. Get your free email at Hotmail"** — 1996
- *Leap:* A line appended to every email users sent.
- *Why it worked:* Every user became a channel. Free plus visible use.
- *Lenses:* The free thing, Media as message.

## The 2000s

**BMW Films, "The Hire"** — Fallon, 2001
- *Leap:* Short films by major directors, released online, with the car as co-star.
- *Why it worked:* Content people sought out, years before branded content was a category.
- *Lenses:* Showmanship.

**Dove, Campaign for Real Beauty** — Ogilvy, from 2004
- *Insight:* Very few women described themselves as beautiful.
- *Leap:* Real women in ads; later "Real Beauty Sketches" (2013), in which a forensic artist draws women as they describe themselves and as strangers describe them.
- *Lenses:* Challenge a belief, Break the wallpaper.

**Dos Equis, "The Most Interesting Man in the World"** — Euro RSCG, 2006
- *Leap:* An absurdly legendary gentleman: "I don't always drink beer. But when I do, I prefer Dos Equis."
- *Lenses:* Character, Exaggeration.

**Apple, "Get a Mac"** — TBWA\Media Arts Lab, 2006
- *Leap:* Two men personifying Mac and PC in conversation.
- *Lenses:* Identity, The enemy, Character.

**Blendtec, "Will It Blend?"** — 2006
- *Leap:* The founder blends phones, marbles, and rakes on camera.
- *Why it worked:* A torture test as an entertaining online serial on almost no budget.
- *Lenses:* Demonstration, Torture test, Format serial.

**Burger King, "Whopper Sacrifice"** — Crispin Porter + Bogusky, 2009
- *Leap:* A Facebook app: unfriend ten friends, get a free Whopper, and the unfriended were notified.
- *Why it worked:* It measured exactly what a friendship was worth.
- *Lenses:* Persuasion principle as idea, The free thing.

**Domino's, "Pizza Turnaround"** — Crispin Porter + Bogusky, 2009–10
- *Problem:* Customers said the pizza tasted like cardboard.
- *Leap:* Rebuild the recipe and broadcast the brutal focus-group feedback.
- *Why it worked:* Honesty plus a real product change.
- *Lenses:* Upstream, Honest apology, Change the product.

## The 2010s and after

**Old Spice, "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like"** — Wieden+Kennedy, 2010
- *Insight:* Women bought much of their partners' body wash.
- *Leap:* A one-take spot addressed to women, then hundreds of personalized video replies to people online within days.
- *Lenses:* The real buyer, Exaggeration, Character.

**Snickers, "You're not you when you're hungry"** — BBDO, 2010
- *Leap:* Hungry people turn into someone else (Betty White playing American football).
- *Why it worked:* A universal truth with an endlessly casting-flexible template.
- *Lenses:* Unsaid truth, Exaggeration, Format serial.

**Patagonia, "Don't Buy This Jacket"** — 2011
- *Leap:* A Black Friday full-page newspaper ad asking customers to buy less.
- *Lenses:* The opposite, Costly signal.

**Coca-Cola, "Share a Coke"** — Ogilvy Sydney, 2011
- *Leap:* Popular first names printed on the bottles.
- *Why it worked:* The pack became personal, giftable, and shareable.
- *Lenses:* Change the product, Reflect them back.

**Metro Trains Melbourne, "Dumb Ways to Die"** — McCann Melbourne, 2012
- *Problem:* Rail safety messages were ignored.
- *Leap:* A catchy song with cute characters dying in silly ways, ending at the trains.
- *Lenses:* Cultural artifact, Showmanship.

**Volvo Trucks, "The Epic Split"** — Forsman & Bodenfors, 2013
- *Leap:* Jean-Claude Van Damme doing the splits between two reversing trucks, demonstrating dynamic steering precision.
- *Why it worked:* A B2B demonstration turned into spectacle.
- *Lenses:* Demonstration, Torture test, Showmanship.

**Always, "#LikeAGirl"** — Leo Burnett, 2014
- *Leap:* Asking people to "run like a girl," then asking young girls the same.
- *Lenses:* Challenge a belief.

**Spotify Wrapped** — from 2016
- *Leap:* Each user's year in listening, made shareable.
- *Why it worked:* Users advertise the brand while talking about themselves.
- *Lenses:* Reflect them back, Format serial.

**State Street, "Fearless Girl"** — McCann New York, 2017
- *Leap:* A bronze girl facing Wall Street's Charging Bull, promoting gender diversity on boards.
- *Lenses:* Cultural artifact, Media as message.

**KFC, "FCK"** — Mother London, 2018
- *Problem:* UK restaurants closed by a chicken shortage.
- *Leap:* An empty bucket with the letters rearranged, in a full-page apology.
- *Lenses:* Honest apology.

**Burger King, "Whopper Detour"** — FCB New York, 2018
- *Leap:* Whoppers for one cent, ordered in the app within 600 feet of a McDonald's.
- *Lenses:* Judo, Media as message.

**Burger King, "Moldy Whopper"** — 2020
- *Leap:* A time-lapse of a Whopper rotting, to prove the removal of artificial preservatives.
- *Lenses:* Costly signal, Demonstration, The opposite.

**Heinz, "Draw Ketchup"** — Rethink, 2021
- *Leap:* People around the world asked to draw ketchup; nearly all drew a Heinz bottle.
- *Lenses:* Let people make it, Hero the product.
