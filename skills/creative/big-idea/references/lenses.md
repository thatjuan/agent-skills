# Lenses

A lens is a generative move. Point it at the fact sheet and an angle, answer its prompt, and write each answer as a one-line idea. Run every lens in step 4; a lens that yields nothing for this brief has still been tried. Each lens names its source in the canon and a campaign that used it.

## Product lenses

| Lens | Prompt | Source | Used by |
|------|--------|--------|---------|
| **The buried fact** | Which fact on the sheet would surprise a customer? Make it the headline. | Ogilvy, Hopkins | Rolls-Royce electric clock |
| **Preemptive claim** | What does the whole category do that nobody says? Say it first. | Hopkins | Schlitz steam-cleaned bottles |
| **Inherent drama** | Where is the romance, tension, or wonder inside how it is made or used? | Burnett | Green Giant, "Harvested in the moonlight" |
| **Demonstration** | How could we prove the claim in one image or act, live, with no words? | Reeves, Hopkins | Volvo Trucks "Epic Split"; Blendtec "Will It Blend?" |
| **Torture test** | What extreme would the product survive that rivals would not? | Reeves | Volvo Trucks "Epic Split" |
| **The USP in seven words** | What single benefit only we can claim, put so plainly a child could repeat it? | Reeves | M&M's "Melts in your mouth, not in your hand" |
| **Flaw as virtue** | What is the product's weakness? Why is it secretly the point? | Bernbach | VW "Think Small"; Guinness "Good things come to those who wait" |
| **Hero the product** | Could the product itself, or its shape, be the whole ad, endlessly varied? | TBWA | Absolut bottle series |
| **Change the product** | What could we change in the product, pack, or experience so the change is the ad? | Wells Lawrence, Schmetterer | Braniff painted planes; Share a Coke |

## Audience lenses

| Lens | Prompt | Source | Used by |
|------|--------|--------|---------|
| **Deprivation** | What happens when they don't have it? Show the absence. | Goodby Silverstein | "Got Milk?" |
| **Unsaid truth** | What do they think but never say out loud? Say it for them. | Sullivan, Trott | Snickers "You're not you when you're hungry" |
| **The real buyer** | Who actually makes the purchase decision, and what do they want? | Wieden+Kennedy | Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" |
| **Customer as hero** | Make the audience the protagonist and the brand the enabler. | Nike | "Just Do It" |
| **Identity** | What does using this say about the user? Give them a badge or a tribe. | Apple, Berger | "Think Different"; "Get a Mac" |
| **Reflect them back** | Show people their own data, words, or behavior in a way they want to share. | Berger (social currency) | Spotify Wrapped; Share a Coke |
| **Challenge a belief** | Which belief the audience holds about itself is quietly harmful? Overturn it. | Dove, Always | Dove "Real Beauty Sketches"; Always "#LikeAGirl" |

## Competitive and category lenses

| Lens | Prompt | Source | Used by |
|------|--------|--------|---------|
| **Break the wallpaper** | List the category's conventions. Do the opposite of each one. | Bernbach, Godin | VW against 1950s Detroit; Dove against beauty advertising |
| **The enemy** | Who or what do we stand against: a rival, a habit, a convention, an idea? | Ries & Trout | Apple "1984"; 7Up "the Uncola" |
| **Number two** | If we are not the leader, how is that our advantage? | Ries & Trout, Bernbach | Avis "We Try Harder" |
| **Own a word** | Which one word could we own in the customer's mind? | Ries & Trout | Volvo, *safety* |
| **New category** | Can we invent a category we are first in? | Ries & Trout | 7Up "the Uncola" |
| **Judo** | How could a rival's size, ad, or presence become our weapon? | Trott | Burger King "Whopper Detour" |
| **Blind test** | Where would a fair head-to-head comparison favor us? Stage it publicly. | Hopkins (testing) | Pepsi Challenge |

## Reframing and behavioral lenses

| Lens | Prompt | Source | Used by |
|------|--------|--------|---------|
| **Upstream** | What is the problem behind the problem? Solve that one. | Trott | Domino's "Our pizza sucks" turnaround |
| **Perception, not reality** | What if we changed how it feels rather than what it is? | Sutherland | Eurostar champagne thought experiment |
| **The opposite** | What is the opposite of the obvious answer, and could it also be right? | Sutherland | Patagonia "Don't Buy This Jacket" |
| **Costly signal** | What act would cost us enough that people believe us? | Sutherland | Burger King "Moldy Whopper"; Patagonia's Black Friday ad |
| **Reframe the price** | Change the frame: per day, per use, against what it replaces, with a decoy. | Ariely, Kahneman | *The Economist* subscription decoy |
| **Loss frame** | What do they lose by not acting? People weigh losses roughly twice as heavily as gains. | Kahneman | "Got Milk?" |
| **The free thing** | What can we give away that costs us little and changes behavior? | Ariely, Hopkins (sampling) | Hotmail's footer; product sampling |
| **Persuasion principle as idea** | Build the whole idea around one of Cialdini's principles: scarcity, social proof, reciprocity, commitment, authority, liking. | Cialdini | Burger King "Whopper Sacrifice" (cost of friendship) |
| **Honest apology** | Where have we failed? Own it, with wit. | Bernbach, Trott | KFC "FCK" |

## Form and fame lenses

| Lens | Prompt | Source | Used by |
|------|--------|--------|---------|
| **Showmanship** | What spectacle would draw a crowd, a story people seek out rather than skip? | Feldwick | BMW Films "The Hire"; Cadbury Smash Martians |
| **Character** | What recurring character could personify the brand for years? | Burnett, Feldwick | Dos Equis "Most Interesting Man in the World" |
| **Exaggeration** | Push the benefit to absurd, literal extremes. | Pricken | Old Spice; Snickers |
| **Metaphor and analogy** | What does the product resemble in another world? Swap it in. | Pricken | Apple "1984" (Orwell's Big Brother) |
| **Visual pun / combination** | Merge the product with an unexpected object to form one image. | Pricken, Lois | Absolut city executions |
| **Cultural artifact** | Could the work become a song, a game, a statue, a meme, a ritual? | Feldwick, Berger | Metro Trains "Dumb Ways to Die"; "Fearless Girl" |
| **Let people make it** | Hand the idea to the audience and let them produce it. | Berger | Heinz "Draw Ketchup" |
| **One shot only** | What would we do if we could run it once, on the biggest stage? | Chiat/Day | Apple "1984," aired nationally once |
| **Media as message** | Could where or when it runs be the idea itself? | Schmetterer | Burger King "Whopper Detour" (geofence); "Fearless Girl" (location) |
| **Format serial** | A fixed template with endless fills, so each new execution is an event. | AMV BBDO | *The Economist* red posters; Absolut |

## Forced combination

After the lenses, take three random pairs from the fact sheet (a product fact with an audience truth, a cultural current with a category convention, a fact with a lens it has not met) and write one idea per pair. Young's whole method lives in this step: the combinations nobody thought to make.
