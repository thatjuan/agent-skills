## Durations

| Duration | Typical use | Expansion | Beats / frames | Hook window | V/O word budget |
|----------|-------------|-----------|----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **6s** | Bumper, OOH digital | 5 lines, often compressed to Conflict → Result | 2–3 | Frame 1 | 12–15 |
| **15s** | Social, pre-roll, cutdown | 5 lines | 4–6 | First 2s | 30–35 |
| **30s** | Broadcast, pre-roll | 5 lines | 7–10 | First 3s | 60–70 |
| **60s** | Brand, prestige broadcast | 10 lines | 12–18 | First 5s | 120–140 |
| **90s–2 min** | Cinema, hero brand film, manifesto | 10 lines | 18–35 | First 5–8s | 180–280 |
| **3–6 min** | Branded content, founder story, short doc | 20 lines in sections | 30–60 | First 15s | Natural pace |
| **8–20 min** | YouTube and creator long-form | 20 lines in sections | Sections of 1–3 min | First 30s | Natural pace |

**Read pace:** about 2.5 words per second for broadcast voiceover, 3–3.5 for conversational dialogue and to-camera delivery, 1.8–2.2 for somber or cinematic reads. A script whose voiceover overruns the budget fails the budget check; cut words, not frames.

## Platforms

- **TV, CTV, streaming (16:9).** 30s and 15s dominate, often non-skippable. Slower builds are tolerated and the brand can arrive late. Legal supers in the last 3s; end frame held about 3s.
- **YouTube pre-roll, skippable.** The hook and the brand land before the skip button at 5s. 15s and 30s are most common.
- **YouTube bumper (6s, non-skippable).** One image, one line, one twist, one logo.
- **YouTube long-form.** The first 30 seconds promise the payoff and decide retention. Open on the Conflict or a glimpse of the Result, then go back to the Situation. Refresh attention every 30–60 seconds with a new question, reveal, or change of scene. Title and thumbnail make the same promise as the hook.
- **TikTok, Reels, Shorts (9:16).** Hook in 1.5s: motion, a face, a bold claim, or a question. Sound-on platforms, but caption everything. A native, handheld, face-to-camera register beats polish.
- **Facebook and Instagram feed (1:1, 4:5).** 6–15s. Mostly watched muted; on-screen text carries the story. The first frame doubles as the autoplay poster, so it must work as a still.
- **LinkedIn (1:1, 16:9).** 30–90s, founder-led, B2B, recruiting. Captions mandatory; an 8–10s hook is acceptable.
- **Cinema (16:9, 2.39:1).** A captive audience with guaranteed sound. Slow openings are rewarded and production expectations are high.
- **OOH digital.** No sound. Loops every 6–15s around a single visual idea. Type and product must read at a distance.

## Format archetypes

Each archetype is a way of filling the five lines.

| Archetype | Length | How it fills the lines |
|-----------|--------|------------------------|
| **Narrative spot** | 30–60s | A character's five lines played straight; the brand enables the Change |
| **Anthem / manifesto** | 60–90s | Situation is the state of the world, Conflict the tension in it, Change the conviction, Result the world transformed. Earn it with specifics and a real point of view |
| **Demo / product hero** | 15–30s | The demonstration is the Change; Result is the benefit on screen. Insert-heavy |
| **Vignette reel** | 30–60s | 4–8 micro-stories of 3–6s, each a line or a tiny five-line arc, under one voiceover or score |
| **Testimonial / UGC** | 15–60s | The customer tells their own five lines to camera; the proof is the Result |
| **Two worlds** | 15–30s | World A is the Situation, World B the Desire, the gap the Conflict |
| **Founder / origin** | 60s–3 min | The founder's own five lines, to camera with B-roll |
| **Creator story** | 5–20 min | The creator's five lines expanded to twenty, in sections; A-roll plus B-roll |
| **Explainer** | 60s–5 min | The viewer is the character: Desire is understanding or the outcome, Conflict is the confusion or the problem, Change is the key idea |
| **Branded documentary** | 2–6 min | Real subjects, little voiceover; the five lines are found in interviews, not written |
| **Mnemonic** | 6–15s | Hook, a repeatable visual and sonic signature, end card; built for frequency |
| **Episodic** | 3–5 parts | Each episode has its own five lines and a thread into the next; the campaign has five lines of its own |

## Aspect ratios and safe zones

| Ratio | Use |
|-------|-----|
| **16:9** | TV, YouTube, OTT, cinema |
| **9:16** | TikTok, Reels, Shorts |
| **1:1** | Feeds, LinkedIn |
| **4:5** | Instagram and Facebook feed |
| **2.39:1** | Scope, prestige brand film |

Keep type and key action within the central 80% of the frame so platform UI (captions, like rails, scrubbers) doesn't cover them.

## Asset suite

Ad campaigns ship as suites: a hero cut (30s or 60s), cutdowns (15s, 6s), vertical and square reframes, stills, loops, sound-off cuts, and localized versions. When scripting the hero cut, flag the anchor frames each cutdown keeps. A 6s usually lives inside the 30s as two or three specific frames, so design them for that.
