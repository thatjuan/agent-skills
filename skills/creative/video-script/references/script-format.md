## Frame schema

Each frame is an H3 with its number and timecode, followed by labeled fields:

```markdown
### Frame [N] — `MM:SS–MM:SS`
**Line:** [Situation | Desire | Conflict | Change | Result]
**Shot:** [size + angle + lens + movement]
**Visual:** [Specific prose: place, light, cast, wardrobe, action, expression, motion in frame.]
**On-Screen:** [Supers, kinetic type, lower thirds, captions, legal]
**V/O:** "[Copy]" *(read direction)*
**Dialogue:** *[Character]* "[Line]" *(delivery)*
**SFX:** [Diegetic and designed sound]
**Music:** [What the music does at this moment]
**Transition:** [How this frame hands off to the next]
**Note:** [Optional: the frame's story job in one sentence]
```

A field that doesn't apply gets `—` instead of being dropped, so each field reads as a column down the script.

---

## Field conventions

- **Timecode:** `MM:SS–MM:SS`, start to end. Timecodes are contiguous and the last one ends at the runtime.
- **Line:** the five-line line the frame serves. A frame between two lines names the one it pushes toward.
- **Shot:** terse, in standard abbreviations: EWS, WS, FS, MWS, MS, MCU, CU, ECU, INS (insert), OTS, POV, 2S. Add angle, focal length, and movement: *"Slow push-in MCU, 35mm, eye-level."*
- **Visual:** full sentences, the longest field. See [Visual Description Craft](visual-description-craft.md).
- **On-Screen:** quote text exactly as it appears, with style when it matters: `"you tried." (kinetic, lowercase sans, bottom third)`.
- **V/O:** copy in double quotes, then the read direction in italics: *(warm, dry, landing the joke flat)*.
- **Dialogue:** character name in italics, line in quotes, delivery in italics. Note the language if it isn't English.
- **SFX:** comma-separated cues, each with a modifier: `kettle whistle (rising), distant traffic, one cat-bell jingle`.
- **Music:** behavior, not just a track: `holds a low pad, drops to silence on the cut`.
- **Transition:** belongs to the outgoing frame. Hard cut is the default; name anything else (match cut, J-cut, L-cut, smash cut, whip pan, dissolve). Cut for emotion first and story second; continuity comes last (Walter Murch).
- **Note:** optional, one sentence, the why.

## Worked example: 30s spot

```markdown
# "First Light" | Riverside Coffee | 30s | 16:9

## Logline
A new mother can't put her baby down long enough to make coffee, so Riverside brings it before she has to ask.

## Five Lines
- **Situation:** At 4:47 a.m., Maya, a new mother in a one-bedroom city apartment, rocks her newborn back to sleep.
- **Desire:** She wants one small thing for herself: a real coffee.
- **Conflict:** Her arms are full, the kitchen is too far, and the baby wakes at the slightest sound.
- **Change:** One thumb-tap on a saved order, and across the dark city a cyclist and a barista start moving for her.
- **Result:** She opens the door to a steaming cup with a marker sun on the lid, the baby asleep on her chest, and takes the first sip of a morning that is hers.

## Brief
- **Audience:** Parents of newborns and under-twos in dense cities. Sleep-deprived, time-starved, finding small dignities where they can.
- **Takeaway:** Riverside gets your coffee to you before you have to ask.
- **Tone:** Warm, observational, a little wry. Sweet but earned. Real, not aspirational.
- **Format:** 30s · YouTube pre-roll and CTV · 16:9, with a 9:16 cutdown
- **Hook:** First 3 seconds: a bottle, a clock reading 4:47, silence.
- **Assumptions:** Riverside's app supports saved orders; the roastery is shootable as-is.

## Treatment
Maya moves through the small choreography of a 4:47 a.m. feed. The apartment is quiet: building hum, a dripping tap, a baby's soft snuffle. Her eyes drift to the coffee machine across the dark kitchen. She shifts her weight to stand, the baby stirs, and she sinks back down. With her free thumb she taps her phone once and sets it face down. Across the city, things start moving: a cyclist clips in on a wet street, and in a warm roastery a barista draws a small sun on a cup lid. The cup arrives on her doormat with no buzzer, only a soft vibration. Maya opens the door with the baby asleep on her chest, sees the sun, and smiles for the first time. She sits, sips, and closes her eyes. "You don't have to ask. We're already on the way." Riverside wordmark, two-note sonic logo.

## Sound
- **Music:** Spare upright piano, right hand only, one note at a time. A melody forms through the Change and resolves into a held warm chord at the door. No drop, no swell.
- **Voiceover:** One line at the end. Woman, late 30s, warm and dry with a slight rasp. 10 words against a 60–70 word budget; the picture carries the story.
- **Sound design:** Naturalistic and quiet. The phone vibration is the only sharp sound. The baby's whimper in Frame 3 is the tension spike.
- **Sonic logo:** Two-note marimba on the end card.

**Transition:** Hard cut.
**Note:** The hook: no baby yet, so the viewer leans in to work out what they're looking at.

### Frame 2 — `00:03–00:06`

**Shot:** Slow push-in MS, handheld, 35mm.
**Visual:** *Maya, late 20s,* sits on the edge of an unmade bed in an oversized oatmeal cardigan over a faded band t-shirt, in socks, hair half-up in a claw clip. Her newborn is against her shoulder; she sways. A bedside lamp throws warm light on one side of her face; the rest of the room is blue moonlight through a sheer curtain. Neither smiling nor frowning, she has the particular tiredness of being needed by someone who can't say thank you yet.
**On-Screen:** —
**V/O:** —
**Dialogue:** —
**SFX:** The baby's snuffly breathing. One car passing far below.
**Music:** The piano adds a second note.
**Transition:** Hard cut on the sway.
**Note:** Save the cat: we're on her side within three seconds.

### Frame 3 — `00:06–00:09`

**On-Screen:** —

**Dialogue:** —

### Frame 9 — `00:25–00:29`

**Shot:** Static MCU, 50mm.
**Visual:** Back on the edge of the bed, where Frame 2 found her, Maya sips with her eyes closed and her shoulders dropping. The baby sleeps. The blue room has gone warm; sunlight reaches across the duvet.
**On-Screen:** —
**V/O:** "You don't have to ask. We're already on the way." *(warm, dry, nearly a whisper)*
**Dialogue:** —
**SFX:** The smallest sip.
**Music:** The piano releases into silence.
**Transition:** Hard cut to the end card.
**Note:** The final image mirrors Frame 2: same bed, same sway, a different morning.

### Frame 10 — `00:29–00:30` — End card
**Line:** Result
**Shot:** Static graphic.
**Visual:** Black frame. The Riverside wordmark centered in a warm-white serif, with the marker sun from the lid beside it. Below, in small handwritten script: *coffee, before you ask*.
**On-Screen:** `RIVERSIDE COFFEE` · *coffee, before you ask* · `riverside.co/app`
**V/O:** —
**Dialogue:** —
**SFX:** —
**Music:** Two-note marimba sonic logo.
**Transition:** Fade to black.

## Production Notes
- **Locations:** A one-bedroom apartment (bedroom, kitchen, hallway, front door); the Riverside roastery as-is; a quiet city street, pre-dawn, on a permitted block.
- **Cast:** Maya (late 20s, real-feeling rather than model casting); a newborn (real or animatronic, depending on schedule); a cyclist (mid-20s); a barista's hands (tattooed forearms).
- **Wardrobe and props:** Maya in an oversized oatmeal cardigan, faded band tee, and socks. The cyclist with a Riverside delivery box and monogram cap. A kraft cup with a white lid and a hand-drawn marker sun.
- **VFX and graphics:** In-app UI comp (Frame 4); end-card typography (Frame 10).
- **Mandatories:** Logo, tagline, and URL on the end card, held about 3s. Sonic logo. A caption track for sound-off platforms.

## Cutdowns
- **15s:** Frames 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10.
- **6s:** Frames 3, 8, 10.
- **9:16:** Reframe Frame 5 so the cyclist enters vertically; Frame 7 becomes a top-down insert of the cup on the doormat.
```

## Variants

### Creator and YouTube videos

The speaker is usually both narrator and character. Write the script as A-roll (to camera) and B-roll (cover footage), grouped into sections that each name their line:

- Use an H2 per section with its line and timecode range, for example `## Section 2 — Conflict — 01:10–03:40`.
- Within a section, alternate `A-roll:` blocks (the spoken copy, verbatim, with delivery notes) and `B-roll:` frames in the standard schema.
- Put the hook (the first 15–30 seconds) in its own section, ahead of the Situation. It previews the Conflict or the Result to promise the viewer a payoff.
- Mark retention beats: a fresh question, reveal, or change of scene roughly every 30–60 seconds.
- Any sponsor or product mention gets its own section and tag (`Line: —`), so the story's trace stays clean.

### Vignette and anthem films

Use one H3 per vignette, which may hold one to four frames. Each vignette is a small five-line story or one line of the larger one. Put the full voiceover first as one continuous block with timecode anchors, then the vignettes.

### Demo and product films

Most frames are inserts. Add a `Product Action:` field above `Visual:` describing exactly what the product does in the shot. Place the demonstration on the Change.

### Founder, testimonial, and documentary

Add a `Subject:` field (name, role, one-sentence bio) and a `Soundbite:` field for verbatim interview lines, separate from `V/O:`. When the story comes from interviews, write the five lines from the material first, then pick the soundbites that carry each line.

### Episodic campaigns

Script each episode in its own document, each with its own five lines. Add a campaign bible that holds the five lines for the whole arc, recurring cast, voiceover voice, sonic logo, end-frame rules, and tagline.
