# Clean Writing

Write clear, economical prose with a natural voice. Invoke `clean-writing` to draft text, tighten a passage, or remove formulaic AI phrasing. It also applies automatically when a writing task fits.

Example: "Use clean-writing to edit this announcement. Keep the facts and our casual tone."

The [skill](./SKILL.md) combines composition principles with a short editing pass. It preserves necessary detail, uncertainty, and the author's voice. It requires no tools or other skills at runtime.

## Sources and editorial choices

- William Strunk Jr. and E. B. White, *The Elements of Style*, fourth edition. [Publisher and contents](https://www.pearson.com/en-gb/subject-catalog/p/elements-of-style-the-pearson-new-international-edition/P200000005508); [text consulted](https://paulimhoff.github.io/files/strunk-white-the-elements-of-style-4th-edition.pdf). White's approach to style informs natural voice, restraint, and revision.
- Strunk's earlier [public-domain text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/37134/37134-h/37134-h.htm), especially the composition chapter. This is the precursor, not the Strunk and White edition.
- [Humanizer by @blader](https://github.com/blader/humanizer), local version 2.1.1. Its checks inform the treatment of inflated significance, vague attribution, stock rhetoric, synonym cycling, and decorative formatting.

This is an original synthesis, not a reproduction of either source. Humanizer's advice to add personality is bounded by fidelity to the author: editing must not invent feelings, facts, or evidence. Pattern checks diagnose weak prose rather than identify its author. The em dash preference is a local convention, not a Strunk and White rule.
